## Something at the Door

The Toad Stompers barred themselves into [Derek Desleigh](/hobbity/world/npcs/#derek-desleigh)'s quarters a little before dawn, laid Derek and his hired blade out on two of the beds, and slept in the other two, taking turns at the door. [Boffo](/hobbity/world/pcs/boffo/) went to bed observing that none of them had seen the sun in a long time, and that they were living like hobbits on the night shift.

Near the end of the last watch, [Wedge](/hobbity/world/pcs/wedge/) heard something on the far side of the door. Not footsteps. Sniffing, and claws, the way a dog digs at a door it wants to be on the other side of. He sat very still and listened. After a few minutes it stopped, and nothing else came.

They woke in what their hobbit stomachs swore was mid-afternoon, mended and rested. Outside the door the mud was churned with prints, and Boffo, crouching over them, pronounced them not reptilian. Something large, with fur. Whatever it was had pawed at the door for a while and then wandered off to try somewhere else.

## A Cellar From All Over the Shire

The first door south was stuck. Boffo put his shoulder to it with no particular plan and it gave all at once, and so did the ceiling. Wedge hopped back in time. [Turnip](/hobbity/world/pcs/turnip/) did not, and was buried to the ankles in fallen earth, with only his feet sticking out. They pulled him out by them. Wedge laid a hand on him and set him right.

The room behind was a wine cellar: fifteen casks and kegs, all raised off the mud and branded. Ten were watery junk any hobbit could tell at a sniff. Four were not. There were two kegs of strong dark beer and two casks of wine that none of them could have afforded before they took up adventuring, some six hundred gold pieces of drink altogether. One cask was the same fine [Orlane](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane) wine Derek had been drinking. The other was branded from a town called Volune. The kegs gave no clue at all.

Boffo read the brands, which was as far as his reading went. "So this cult is everywhere," he said.

## Supplies for a Small Army

They passed a pool of deep blue water so thick with tiny fish that it glittered in the lantern light, and Boffo caught the troglodyte smell again, stronger than before. Somewhere behind them something went into the pool with a great splash. Then nothing. Wedge said that if there was going to be a fight, he would rather it weren't in the mud, and they moved on.

The door to the west wore a big iron padlock. Wedge's picks got nowhere. Turnip's got a click, and the padlock dropped open into his hand. He kept it.

Boffo opened the door, on the principle that he was the door man even if he couldn't pick one. Behind it was the best-kept room in the dig: crates and barrels under oilcloth against the damp, sacks of beets, beans, wheat, cornmeal, potatoes, and turnips, jars of oil, lard, and salt, boxes of spikes and hammers, timber stacked for building, four shovels, barrels of torches, and racks of spears, daggers, short swords, and leather armour, all oiled and in order. It was enough food and steel for a small army.

"They're gearing up for something," said Wedge.

## Fellow Cultists

Boffo was standing in the doorway when the torchlight came round the corner: two hobbits in grey robes, carrying sacks. They stopped dead and looked at him, and he looked at them, and for one long moment nobody knew what to do.

Boffo shut the door in their faces. It was the only thing that came to him.

On the far side someone shouted. Then, after a while, someone knocked.

Now, Boffo's chainmail had come out of [Misha](/hobbity/world/npcs/#misha)'s bedroom under the [Temple of Merikka](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane), and it had been cut for a priestess, not a miller. On his arm was the shield painted with Merikka's crest. In the torchlight, to a hobbit who wasn't thinking very hard, he looked like one of their own. Boffo had already taken two potatoes from a sack to fill out the front of the mail.

"Come in," he trilled, in his best lady's voice.

The first one came in slowly. His face was blank, and his eyes had the look the Toad Stompers had last seen on the charmed acolytes in Orlane, a look with nobody much behind it. Something was not quite right, and he could tell, and he drew himself up to fight.

"Be a dear," said Boffo, "and help us find the extra cult robes, fellow cultists."

The hobbit relaxed. "Just here for the food," he said, and went off to fill his sack. The second never registered the Toad Stompers at all; he was scooping beans as if in his sleep. Wedge made a show of rummaging in a crate. Then the three of them strolled out and Turnip snapped the padlock shut behind them. A little while later, from down the passage, they heard it rattle.

Wedge gave Boffo a high five. "We didn't have to kill anybody," he said.

## The Punt

Further on, the mess hall door stood open and lit, and more of the robed hobbits sat at the filthy table, paying the passing Toad Stompers no mind at all. They walked straight past the goblins the party had dumped in the water without a glance. Two more went by with a torch, heading south. Wedge wondered whether they were going down to see the boss, and the Toad Stompers followed until the torch slipped away from them in the dark.

So they went back for the boat. They had found it the night before behind a secret door, when a glint caught the lantern. It was no rowboat but a flat punt pushed along with a pole, with room for ten. Boffo, as the strongest, took the pole. Wedge stood in the bow with the lantern.

Off to the east something went into the water with a splash, and then all was quiet again.

They put in at a sandy beach to the southwest. Along the whole west side of the cavern the roof had come down in one great collapse, as though something much bigger had once lain beyond it.

## Where the Dead Go

A door at the back of the beach was swollen shut. Turnip heaved at it and got nowhere; Boffo heaved and it burst open, and the smell of death came out.

The lantern found water, and in the water, and heaped up out of it, dozens upon dozens of dead: troglodytes, goblins, hobbits, and other things none of them could put a name to. Nothing moved. Water dripped.

"This is where they throw their dead," said Wedge.

"This is so un-hobbit-like," said Boffo.

## Feeding the Crocodiles

They were climbing back into the punt when Turnip saw the log in the water was watching them.

"Guys," he said. "Something's coming."

It was a crocodile, swimming lazily, minding its own business with great attention. They backed up the beach. Wedge reckoned that if it came for them, land was the best place to meet it. They had fed this crocodile, or one of its cousins, a whole goblin only the night before.

There was no shortage of food behind them. Boffo went back into the room of the dead and came out dragging a troglodyte's leg, taken off at the hip by his new short sword. It was nasty, grisly work, and the leg was the size of a hobbit. He spun round twice and let fly, and it went sploosh into the water a long way out.

The crocodile took it. A second crocodile, right behind the first, swam past the free meal and crawled up onto the beach. Wedge put an arrow into the top of its jaw. It made a noise none of them had known crocodiles made, thought better of the whole business, and backed into the water and away, the arrow standing out of its head like a flagpole.

"The other one's got something to eat," said Wedge. "Back in the boat."

## What Scratched at the Door

They poled along the south wall, through water clear enough to show the bottom and the crocodiles keeping a careful distance, to a landing where a boat had plainly been tied up before. In the mud were the prints of hobbits, and of troglodytes, and over the top of all of them the same big pawprints that had been outside the door that morning.

Then Turnip felt the hair rise on the back of his neck. It was the old, low feeling of being hunted. He turned, and just at the edge of the light two eyes caught the lantern.

"Oh no," he said. "There's something there."

Boffo shuffled round to put himself between the eyes and his friends. Turnip slung a stone into the dark. Wedge moved the lantern up, and they saw it: a weasel, but a weasel nine feet long, nose to tail, that had followed their scent across the dig.

Wedge's arrow missed. Turnip's next stone took it square between the eyes, and it didn't care. It leaped, landed in front of Boffo, and sank its teeth into his shoulder through the mail. Then it hung on and began to drink.

"This thing's going to kill me," said Boffo, with some justice.

He hacked at its face with his short sword, and it only bit down harder and started dragging him back toward the dark, away from the others. Weasels do not let go. Wedge's darts of light went into it, twice. Turnip's whip missed, then cracked across its back and lit the water behind them blue-white, and the air smelled of a summer storm. Boffo's arm was going numb and his head was going light. He swung once more with all the luck he had left, and the weasel died with its teeth still in him. It slumped off him slowly, and the blood ran down his arm and chest.

"Die, you bloodsucking weasel," said Boffo, a little late.

He called up a cure for himself, and Turnip laid a hand on him for good measure. Behind them the crocodiles, drawn by the blood, came up out of the water for the weasel, and the Toad Stompers left them to it.

## Pews

The troglodyte smell came on strong as they went, and then the lantern guttered out. Wedge poured in the last of his oil.

A door stood half open on loose hinges, and the stink came out of it. Inside were three wooden benches, battered and mismatched, arranged before nothing in particular.

"Pews," said Boffo. "P-U."

Turnip studied the joinery and concluded that the benches had come flat in a sack from some goblin carpenter and been put together by someone who had lost half the pegs.

That was when the troglodyte in the corner, which had been the exact colour of the corner, stopped being part of the wall and bit Boffo on his good shoulder from behind. A second sprang at Wedge out of the dark and raked him.

## Holding the Door

Boffo told the other two to back out of the door, and he backed out after them, step by step, with the troglodyte on him all the way. In the doorway he stopped, and there he stayed.

Luck deserted all three of them at once. Wedge's arrows went into the dark. Turnip's stones went wide. The troglodytes stank so foully that the hobbits' eyes streamed, and they clawed and bit, and the claws found their way through Boffo's mail time after time. He tried to drag the door shut and a troglodyte was standing in it. He split that one in half with his short sword.

"I don't like that," said Boffo. "That's some bad stew."

The second faded off into the dark. A moment later they heard it hammering on some far door, and then there were more of them, coming up the passage in a line. Turnip swung the statue's whip and a bolt of lightning ran the whole length of them.

"Merikka," said Boffo, "I pray for average luck."

Merikka did not answer. A troglodyte reached him and opened him up in three places. Wedge read the last healing scroll over him, and asked whether it was time to run for it, and answered himself: if they ran, the troglodytes would follow, and the only hope was the doorway, one at a time. So Boffo stood in it and cut the next one down with a single stroke, and bit deep into the one after, and Wedge's arrow finished it.

Then Boffo slammed the door and held it shut.

For a moment the last troglodyte heaved at it from the other side. Then the heaving stopped. In its place came a wet, tearing, squelching sound, and they understood that it had found its dead friends and stopped caring about anything else.

Boffo would not let go of the handle until it was tied. Turnip's chisel went into the frame and Boffo's rope went round the handle, because Turnip was not about to sacrifice his good rope to a troglodyte.

"We need to become the Trog Stompers," said Boffo.

"I'd say we're halfway there," said Wedge.

## Wedge Has Had Enough

They took the punt back across the bloody water, where the weasel lay in pieces on the bottom and the crocodiles drifted, fat and content. Boffo shoved it off and the three of them ran and leaped aboard together to give it a good start. Back in Derek's quarters they barred the door, poured the wine, and sat round the table.

The room was getting ripe. Nobody said much for a while.

"We got lucky getting this far," Wedge said at last. "Everyone knows we're here. I can't imagine it gets any easier the deeper we go. If we do find what we're after down there, I think we'll regret finding it."

Boffo said they had come to find [Zacharias](/hobbity/world/npcs/#zacharias). Wedge said he had also come to live, and that he did not give two hoots for Zacharias if he had to fight through an army of troglodytes to reach him. Whatever was happening to Zacharias could go on happening.

But he had a proposal. They had found the cult's home. They had found Derek and his hired blades, and the goblins, and the charmed hobbits, and the troglodytes who served them. That was something to carry back to Orlane, to [Constable Grover](/hobbity/world/npcs/#constable-grover) and to [Ramne](/hobbity/world/npcs/#ramne), along with the plain admission that three hobbits could not finish it alone. He had no wish to hurt the hobbits in the grey robes. They were somebody's family. He would rather come back with help, and with whatever it took to wake them up.

"And I'll tell you this," said Wedge. "I can't do it with three arrows. That's a hundred percent true."

Boffo put on his half-moon spectacles and studied him over the top of them. "That's only eighty-five percent true," he said.

There were arrows in the storeroom. Nobody went back for them.

Boffo wondered whether they might at least bring one of the robed hobbits home to see if Ramne could undo what had been done to him. Wedge asked Turnip for his manacles. Turnip had left them on the pony.

## A Snake in His Pocket

Somewhere in all this Boffo turned out his pockets and found the jointed wooden worm he had picked up in Zacharias's study back in Orlane, on the night the statues came alive. He had been fiddling with it when everything went wrong, and stuffed it in a pocket, and forgotten it.

He set it on the table beside an open bottle. It stirred, and wound itself round the bottle's neck, and stoppered it.

It was not a worm. It was a snake.

## A Note for Zacharias

Before they left, Boffo dictated and Wedge wrote. The note went on Derek's table:

_We've dealt with your captors, and look forward to rendezvousing with you in Orlane._

Boffo also arranged Derek's two hired blades so that they appeared to have stabbed each other, and put the jade chest in a big sack and slung it over his shoulder. Derek's things were known in Orlane, and the chest was Zacharias's, and Wedge wanted something in hand that no constable could wave away. The good casks stayed where they were. Wedge would hear of no side trips.

Then they went up the stairs and out of the pit, past the green lady and over the dike, and into the rushes: a day's wading to get clear of the [Rushmoors](/hobbity/world/places/#the-five-shires), and half a day's hard walking after that to Orlane.

On the way, they talked it through. Zacharias's house still had its three living statues in it, a big stone cat among them, and his study. Grover had come back from the coast with two hired deputies who were not from Orlane, one of whom had died at Zacharias's door, and nobody had yet asked Grover why. Grover was the only other hobbit in town who could hold a line, and the dig had coin enough in it to hire more.

"We are the most unconvincing of parties," said Boffo. "The only convincing we can do is with coin."

## Conclusion

The Toad Stompers slept a night under the dig, talked their way past two of the cult's own, and killed a giant weasel and three troglodytes at a heavy cost in blood and luck. Spent, low on arrows, and no nearer the way down, they turned for Orlane with the jade chest, a note left behind for Zacharias, and a list of questions for Constable Grover.
