## The Wood Lets Them Go

On the third morning the trees thinned, and then stopped, and the Toad Stompers walked out of the [Dimwood](/hobbity/world/places/#the-five-shires) into a field of wheat.

They stood in it for a while, on the sensible grounds that a hobbit who has spent two days being handed back to himself by a forest is entitled to a moment in the sun. South of them, a mile or so off, sat the low roofs and the burnt gap of [Orlane](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane). It had been there the whole time. They had walked to it by tunnel twice and never once known where it was.

[Wedge](/hobbity/world/pcs/wedge/) took a long look at the countryside and pronounced it charted. [Turnip](/hobbity/world/pcs/turnip/) announced that the party was good for a fortnight, on the strength of a fishing rod.

Then they turned their backs on the wheat and went north into rougher country, looking for a road that troglodytes had walked.

## The Boar at the Dry River

They found the riverbed instead: a wide stony scar with the water gone out of it and one green pond left at the bottom, and standing in the pond, up to its knees and entirely content, a boar the size of a wheelbarrow.

The three of them were sixty feet above it on the rocks, and would have stayed there, except that the branch Turnip was leaning on cracked like a dropped plate. The boar came out of the water at a flat run.

Wedge's dart of light burst against its shoulder. Turnip's stone went into its neck. [Boffo](/hobbity/world/pcs/boffo/) braced a borrowed spear against the rock and set the butt in the dirt—"for the first time in my life," he said, to nobody, in the tone of a hobbit discovering he has been carrying a tool he never read the instructions for—and the boar ran onto it and did most of the work itself. It went down thrashing in the shallows and stained the pond a colour the pond had not been.

Wedge, who knew his business, had it hung and gutted before the light went. A hundred pounds of good meat came off it. The tusks came off too, because tusks are the sort of thing you show people later.

They dragged the whole business two hundred feet from the carcass, built a fire big enough to argue with, and discovered that a hundred pounds of pork does not walk itself. So they cut poles and lashed a litter, and Boffo took the ropes over his shoulders, and that is how they went on northwest: with a hobbit pulling a sledge of meat through the trees like a very small, very determined pony.

## Barry Comes Through the Camp

The bear arrived at a dead run with three troglodytes behind it.

It was black and enormous and it wanted nothing from them but the far side of the clearing, and in the half-second anyone had to think about it, Wedge named it Barry. Everyone was equally surprised. Wedge asked, hopefully, whether his whistle might make a friend of it. It would not. It had never made a friend of anything.

Wedge and Turnip went into the underbrush and ceased, for practical purposes, to exist. Boffo did not, because Boffo was standing in the open in the middle of a rope harness attached to a hundred pounds of pork, and there is no bush in the world that will hide that.

The troglodytes killed Barry in front of him. Three spears went in and the bear turned and took one of them off his feet, and the thing came up under it with no spear at all and tore the bear's throat out with its hands. Then it looked around the clearing for something else to do that to.

## I Eat Your Stew

What happened next was quick and went badly in almost every particular before it went well.

Wedge's bush declined to hide him and he took a spear through the shoulder for it. Turnip, up a tree, threw his net at the nearest troglodyte and caught Boffo, who was now a hobbit in a harness in a net, and who had some remarks about it. Turnip dropped out of the tree, put a dart from his blowgun into a troglodyte's neck, and watched it fold up in the leaves.

The stench of the things was enough to make a hobbit's eyes water and his arm forget the way to a target. Boffo held his breath, waded out of the netting, and brought the cobra-headed mace flat across the skull of the one that had killed the bear. It came apart with a wet crack and Boffo shouted the thing he had lately taken to shouting, which was:

"I eat your stew!"

"You mess with the Toad Stompers," Wedge called from the ground, bleeding, "you get stomped."

The last one did not care. Troglodytes do not break and do not run; hate does the work that courage does in other creatures. It came through the fire at Boffo and got past the scale mail twice, and for a long moment there was a very real chance that the party's cook was going to end face-down in his own supper. Boffo spent the last of his luck staying upright.

Then a sling stone went between the thing's eyes from thirty feet away and it sat down and did not get up. Turnip lowered the sling and looked mildly embarrassed about it.

## Skinning Barry

They patched each other in the firelight, which by now was a well-practised routine and involved less screaming than it used to.

The troglodyte spears were crude, ugly, and worth precisely nothing, so they went in the fire. Barry got better treatment: they took his pelt, whole and black and heavier than any of them expected, and left the rest of him for whatever the woods sent next. The boar meat stayed on the litter. Nobody was giving up the boar meat.

One useful thing came out of the whole business. Boffo had now smelled a troglodyte at close range, at length, and with his whole face, and would never again fail to know when one had walked somewhere. The trail out of the clearing was suddenly a good deal easier to read.

It went northwest.

## Into the Rushmoors

The wood got darker and lower and wetter, and then it stopped being a wood.

The ground gave first—soft, then soft in a way that took a foot up to the ankle and didn't want to give it back. The trees stood in water. Wedge, who had opinions about literature, said it reminded him of somewhere out of an old story where the dead lie under the pools and you are not supposed to look at them, and everyone told him to stop.

Ahead of them the marsh opened out: rushes in thick stands, patches of scummy water between, one to two feet of warm water and slime underfoot and no dry line anywhere in it. The Toad Stompers went in at half speed with their toes finding every root. The litter, which had been a sledge, became a raft, which was an improvement nobody had planned for.

They made an island at dusk—a hump of dry ground the size of a kitchen—and built a small fire in a hole where the marsh couldn't see it.

## The Turtle on Turnip's Watch

Turnip was on watch when the water beside the island rose up and became a head.

It came on ten feet of neck out of the black, a beak on it that could take a hobbit in half at the waist, and it went for him before he had finished understanding it was there. Turnip went sideways, screamed the camp awake, and did the only thing a hobbit with a net does: threw the net.

It caught. For one glorious moment the largest thing any of them had ever seen was tangled in the same net that had lately caught Boffo. It tore free the next moment, but that moment was enough for Wedge to get to the litter, drag out a whole boar leg, and throw it.

The turtle looked at three small shouting hobbits. Then it looked at forty pounds of pork lying in the shallows. Then it took the pork and settled down in the mud to eat, and paid them no further attention whatsoever.

They watched it chew for a while from the far side of a very small island.

"I think we just fed the biggest turtle in the world," Boffo said.

"You have to eat," said Wedge. "An army marches on its stomach."

In the morning everyone was a little mended and nobody had been eaten, and Wedge went round topping them all up anyway, on the grounds that there might be a bigger turtle.

## Three Kinds of Footprints

Swamp is slow country. A day of wading bought them what an hour of road would have.

But somewhere in the middle of it they came onto a path—a real one, trodden and rutted and used—and in the mud of it, plain as a written page, three kinds of print. Hobbit feet. Goblin feet. And the splayed, webbed, froglike marks the Toad Stompers had been following since the [Golden Grain](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane) cellar.

All of them going the same way. All of them going northwest.

## The Fort in the Marsh

At the end of that day the Toad Stompers came out of the rushes and found that someone had built an island.

A dike ringed it—cut stone and timber and packed mud, thrown up out of the marsh and holding the water back—and inside the ring the ground had been dug out three levels deep, so that the bottom of it sat well below the waterline of the swamp around it. A rampart ran along the top of the dike. Torchlight moved down in the pit. Everything was slick and green and smelled of standing water.

On the south side a crane stood over a ramp, its pulleys broken and its rope hanging in the water, with smashed crates scattered under it and three good skiffs tied up at the bottom. Two more had sunk where they were moored. Something had been going in and out of this place for a long time, and lately it had been going in and out in a hurry.

It was late afternoon, and in the Rushmoors that is night. The hobbits lay in the reeds and looked at it with eyes that do not need a torch, and counted goblins.

## The Green Lady

The first thing that made sense of the place was standing in the pit, out of position, tilted, half-roped and abandoned where the ropes had failed.

A statue of [Explictica](/hobbity/world/npcs/#explictica): a woman's body, a serpent's coil, snakes about her head, cut from a single block of marble six feet high. Someone had painted her green, and recently, and better than anyone had painted the jade horror under [Orlane](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane).

"Honey lady," said Wedge, and then, after a moment, "that's a nicer statue than we've seen before."

The things in her hands were not carved. A real whip hung from her fist and swung a little in the wet air. A real sword caught the torchlight along its edge. Two more objects sat in her grip that no chisel had made.

Boffo looked at the crane, and the crates, and the dug-out pit, and the statue standing halfway to somewhere it had never arrived, and got there all at once.

"Oh," he said. "So this is an old temple of Explictica, and they're _excavating_ it."

"They're excavating it," Wedge agreed.

It explained the goblins, who work for whoever is doing the digging. It explained the skiffs and the wreckage on the ramp. It did not explain why a cult that had just been driven out of a village would be hauling a marble goddess up out of a swamp in the dark, and none of them liked the shapes that question made.

## Feeding the Crocodile

There was a goblin asleep in the crook of a tree that grew out of the top of the dike.

Wedge handed Boffo his pack, went into the water without a sound, swam the channel, climbed the slick stone with his fingers and his bare toes, and cut the goblin's throat in its sleep. It never woke.

Turnip, meanwhile, had seen something long and low slide off the bank into the water, and tried to convey _crocodile_ to Boffo entirely with his hands. Boffo watched the performance carefully, concluded it was a vertical clap, and clapped back correctly.

Wedge and Turnip lowered the dead goblin down the outside of the dike and put its head in the water. The crocodile took it and went under, and the swamp was quiet again.

"That's really handy for cleaning up your goblins, eh?" said Wedge, wiping his dagger. "All right, fellas. How do you want to go at these goblins here?"

There were five left. Two on the north rampart throwing knucklebones. Two more sitting to the west with a jug between them. One on the south side, cheerfully making water off the dike and throwing rocks at the crocodile.

Boffo, it should be said, had no way to hit any of them from a distance, having given his bow away and left his sling in the temple back in Orlane. He mentioned this several times.

## Six Goblins on the Rampart

They went round the top of the dike sunwise, low and slow, on stone that gave a hobbit far less cover than honest undergrowth.

The south goblin finished its business, turned, and looked straight at Boffo. Boffo looked straight back. And then Wedge's arrow went through its eye from forty feet and it went off the dike into the water without a splash, and the crocodile had its second supper of the evening.

The alert one on the north rampart noticed the gap where a comrade should have been. It kicked its lazy neighbour awake and opened its mouth to raise the camp—and Turnip dropped five feet onto the rampart and put a stone through the lazy one's eye socket before either of them made a sound. Boffo arrived a heartbeat later and caved in a corpse.

That was the end of quiet. The pair with the jug came up with their weapons and came on, and Turnip did not move an inch and did not stop slinging, and both of them died on the rampart within a breath of each other.

Boffo spent the whole fight trying to reach somebody. "Let me try," he said, jogging along the wall with the mace over his shoulder while goblins fell down in front of him. "Why can't I even try? How am I going to learn if you won't let me try?"

The last one had more spine than the whole rest of the garrison put together. It came at Wedge with a club swinging and Wedge ducked under it, and asked, conversationally, whether there was some manner of running leap that would put him behind the thing.

"I think that's more of a dancing step," said Boffo, arriving at last. "But you do you."

They killed it between them. Six goblins on the dike and not a scratch among the three of them; and below, in the pit, the torches went on burning, and the green lady stood tilted in her ropes, waiting for someone to come down.

## Conclusion

The Toad Stompers have crossed the Rushmoors, learned that anything large in a swamp can be bought off with meat, and taken the rampart of the cult's fortification without losing a hobbit. Beneath them lies a dug-out temple of Explictica, three levels deep and lit from within, and no one left alive on the walls to say they are coming.
