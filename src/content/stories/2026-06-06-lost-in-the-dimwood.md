## Torchlight Coming Back

[Wedge](/hobbity/world/pcs/wedge/) was halfway across the grass, picks in hand, when the torchlight turned round and came back.

He went flat and stayed flat. The troglodytes came into the courtyard in a loose, cheerful crowd, and behind them, on a rope, they had a lizard—a lean grey thing the size of a pony, all shoulder and jaw, hauling at its tether. They walked it up to [Gammy Boffin](/hobbity/world/npcs/#gammy-boffin) until it could very nearly reach him, and then hauled it back, and laughed the way troglodytes laugh. They did this three times. The old hobbit stood on his chain with his fists closed and said nothing at all.

Then they got bored and wandered off, and left the lizard staked out at the far wall to think about its supper.

## Two Swallows of Nothing

Wedge came crawling back and produced a small vial from his pack. He had been saving it for exactly the sort of moment nobody plans for, and this was it.

He drank half and went out of the world. The grass bent under nothing. [Turnip](/hobbity/world/pcs/turnip/), watching from the stair with his bow half-drawn, found the whole business deeply upsetting and said so quietly, twice.

The lock gave in a few heartbeats. Wedge got the vial to Gammy's hand and told him, in the smallest voice a hobbit can make, to drink it all and walk where he was pushed. Gammy drank, vanished, and immediately began to say thank you. Two invisible hands took him by the collar and steered him at speed across the grass while the lizard turned its head and stared at a courtyard with nothing in it.

The troglodyte on the wall never looked round.

## Down the Long Dark

They put a mile of tunnel between themselves and the ruin, stooped and crab-walking the whole way, the lantern shuttered low. Gammy did the talking, being unable to stop. He had been lifted out of [Gomwick](/hobbity/world/places/#gomwick)—out of his own home, in the small hours, with the door shut politely behind him—and walked for days on his own two feet with a bag over his head. He had not seen his daughter since. He asked, more than once, whether Daisy was all right, and nobody had an answer for him.

[Whiskers](/hobbity/world/npcs/#whiskers), awake at last and furious about it, rode on Wedge's head and watched the dark for something to kill.

They came up through the cellar of the burnt [Golden Grain](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane) into daylight, and handed Gammy over to [Belba](/hobbity/world/npcs/#belba) at the [Slumbering Serpent](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane), who took one look at him and started heating water. Word went east to [Daisy](/hobbity/world/npcs/#daisy-boffin) that her father was alive.

## Buying Rope Again

They spent what was left of the day on the unglamorous business of going back. Oil, torches, arrows, a fresh coil of rope. Boffo went round to [Killian Gade](/hobbity/world/npcs/#killian-gade)'s stable to look in on [Opal](/hobbity/world/npcs/#opal), who was glossy and content and a very good girl, and who would not be coming into any tunnel.

Then they went down under the inn and walked the mile again, and came up into the courtyard at dusk. It was empty. The stake stood alone with its broken chain, the lizard gone, the torchlight gone, the trail cold as a cellar floor.

Northwest, then, into the [Dimwood](/hobbity/world/places/#the-five-shires).

## The Wood Declines

[Ramne](/hobbity/world/npcs/#ramne) had told them the woods turned him around. He had made it sound like a nuisance.

For two days the Toad Stompers walked northwest and arrived, repeatedly, somewhere else. Turnip marked trees with chalk and found his own marks ahead of him when they should have been behind. Boffo watched moss grow on whichever side of the trunk it felt like growing on. Wedge took a bearing off the sun at noon and got a different answer an hour later, and swore at the sky about it. Twice they crossed a stream running the wrong way for the country it was in. The wood did not menace them. It simply, patiently, put them back where they had come from, the way a shopkeeper puts a thing back on a shelf.

By the second evening they had covered a great deal of ground and gone nowhere, and were reduced to arguing about whether a fallen elm was the same fallen elm.

## Something on the Second Watch

The dead came for them in the small hours.

Three of them, walking out of the trees without any hurry at all, and nothing on the wind to give them away—Whiskers, who could find a snake through a foot of mud, never stirred. Turnip's arrow went into a chest and the chest kept coming with the shaft still in it. Wedge threw a dart of light that punched a hole clean through a shoulder, and that one kept coming too. So Boffo stepped up and swung the cobra-headed mace flat across a jaw and took the head off the neck in a wet crack, and the body walked two more paces before it worked out it was finished.

They fought the other two down between the fire and the trees. It was ugly work with no cleverness in it, only hitting things until they came apart. When it was done, the three of them stood over the pieces getting their breath back, and none of the faces belonged to anyone they knew, which was a relief nobody said out loud.

They burned what was left and nobody slept much after.

## Conclusion

Gammy Boffin is safe at the Slumbering Serpent, and the cult's reach into Gomwick is no longer a guess. But the trail out of the courtyard went cold, the Dimwood spent two days politely handing the Toad Stompers back to themselves, and something in these trees is putting the dead on their feet.
