## Down Into the Dig

The Toad Stompers went down into the pit a little after midnight, with the rampart cleared behind them and nobody below any the wiser.

The green lady was the first thing they reached. [Turnip](/hobbity/world/pcs/turnip/) took the whip out of her fist, and found it crackled. [Boffo](/hobbity/world/pcs/boffo/) took the sword, which had been forged for someone twice his size, and left his cobra-headed mace in the empty hand. Below her the dig became tunnels: mud walls shored with timbers swollen fat with damp, water dripping from every ceiling, the stink of swamp and lizard and troglodyte over everything. A few goblins met them on the way in and stayed where they fell.

At the bottom of all that mud they found a door that was well kept and barred from the inside, and behind it [Derek Desleigh](/hobbity/world/npcs/#derek-desleigh) and two hard-faced hobbits who had been paid to keep the goblins in line. Two of the three died there, Derek among them. The third was tied to one of his own chairs.

## Derek's Quarters

It was the nicest room any of them had seen in a month. The rest of the dig was mud and drip, but this was dry and snug: a fire in the hearth, comfortable beds, crockery, good food, and a barrel of wine every bit as fine as the bottle Boffo had been served on his first night in [Orlane](/hobbity/world/places/#orlane). There were three tables, each set with its back to the wall, because the three who ate at them had not trusted one another an inch.

They shared out the spoils over a cup of that wine. Derek's leather armour went to [Wedge](/hobbity/world/pcs/wedge/), and Boffo observed that he was putting on the stench of Derek Desleigh. Wedge said he would rinse it in wine to give it a nice fruity smell. The prisoner's short sword went to Boffo, who said a small prayer over his shovel and set it down for good.

"I think this is when I became a real adventurer," he said, "when I threw down my shovel and picked up a sword." The big sword went across his back on a leather strap, more for show than for use.

## The Jade Chest

In Derek's bedroom, on a table, sat a chest the size of a bread box, carved from a single piece of jade with snakes twined all round it.

Boffo, who had decided he was the party's man for traps, found the one on it at once. Then he set it off. A puff of something sweet went into his face and he froze, bent double, nose an inch from the lock. For several long minutes Wedge and Turnip discussed what to do with him while he stood there like a garden ornament, until the feeling crept back into his fingers and he was able, stiffly, to stand up.

His first thought, on regaining the use of his tongue, was that the party ought to carry a little barrow for moving paralysed hobbits about.

The lock beat Wedge's picks, and it beat Turnip's. So they carried the chest out to the prisoner and set it in front of him.

## The Job Interview

His name was [Ben Griff](/hobbity/world/npcs/#ben-griff), and he was very polite about being tied to a chair.

He had no key. If he had to guess, he said, [Zacharias](/hobbity/world/npcs/#zacharias) had it, because the chest was Zacharias's. Zacharias had brought it there himself. No, he did not sleep in these rooms. He went _downstairs_, where Ben was not permitted to go and had never wished to.

The question of what to do with him followed. Boffo pointed out that they were all hobbits at the end of the day. Turnip asked whether he would like to join the party. Boffo suggested half a share. And so, without anyone quite deciding to, the Toad Stompers interviewed Ben Griff for a position.

His duties, as he described them, were to get up in the morning, climb all the way up to the surface to relieve himself, since there was no privy below, and kick the goblins awake to make sure they were on watch, which they plainly had not been. How many goblins? He did not count them. More than one and fewer than a thousand. They bred faster than you could kill them.

Wedge asked where he saw himself in five years.

"Somewhere a long way from here," said Ben Griff. "A seaside town. My feet up, a glass of wine, the sun going down, and a good dinner on its way."

His greatest weakness, he admitted, was not knowing when to shut up and let the fellow holding the knife do the talking. Wedge tapped twice and scratched his nose, which was the signal they had agreed on, and the interview was over.

## Turnip's Chisel

Turnip had a chisel in his pack, as Turnip generally does, and he worked the jade lid so neatly that the catch still closed afterwards.

Inside lay a little snake-headed mace no longer than a dagger, more ornament than weapon. Beneath it was a black robe, folded neatly, bearing a crimson symbol that every one of them knew from somewhere and none of them could place. And there was a scroll case shaped like a serpent with a head at either end, each head a stopper. In it were two scrolls of healing.

"So it's a cultist's starter kit," said Boffo. He thought that one of them was going to have to dress up as a cultist sooner or later. Being unable to read, he left the scrolls to the other two.

They left the chest by the fire. A jade box is worth a great deal to the right buyer, but the right buyer would be someone starting a snake cult of his own.

## Letting Ben Go

They decided not to kill him in cold blood. They untied his feet, kept his wrists bound, and walked him to the foot of the stairs.

There Boffo asked about the passage that branched off to the right, and Ben Griff looked sheepish.

"That's where the others are," he said. "Her pets. The ones that have been touched by the goddess. They go down, and they come back up, or they get brought in from the villages. All they do is pray." He did not know the goddess's name. He did not ask questions. "Just don't go to the right," he said. "There's terrible things down there. Save yourselves the trouble."

Turnip told him that it sounded like exactly the sort of thing he ought to have stepped in and stopped, and that a hobbit had to make things his business. Wedge said he hoped never to see him again, and that if he did, he hoped to find him leading a useful life among decent folk rather than lurking in tunnels with goblins. Then he planted a foot in the seat of Ben Griff's breeches and sent him up the stairs.

"That was our good deed for the day," said Boffo. "Only two more to go. One each."

## The Doors on the South Wall

Boffo thought they ought to listen to him. Wedge thought he was full of it. Then they went right.

The passage ran east along a row of plain wooden doors. Turnip put his ear to the first and heard a single voice saying the same thing over and over, flat and low, as if it had been saying it for a very long time. Behind the next he heard snoring. Wedge eased that door open a crack: four beds, four sleeping shapes under the covers, and one in a red bed snoring loud enough to rattle the crockery. He shut it again, very gently.

Turnip wanted to bar every door from the outside. Wedge wanted to find the way down. Wedge won.

## The Viper in the Rafters

Boffo was saying, to nobody in particular, that he hoped the place had not been dug out for one enormous snake, when he looked up and saw one.

It was not enormous. It was five feet of pit viper coiled on a rafter right over his head, and it had not seen them first. Turnip threw his net at it and the net could find nothing to grab. His sling stone missed. Boffo, with his new sword, opened it up along the coil. The viper struck at him and its fangs skidded off his chainmail, and then Wedge put an arrow through it and it dropped off the rafter. The arrow did not survive the business, and neither did the one before it.

## The Door With the X

Further on was a door with a great black X slapped across it in tar. The mud in front of it held hobbit prints, and other prints none of them knew.

"Doesn't that mean plague?" said Boffo.

Turnip thought it might mean the forbidden way down, the one Ben Griff had not been allowed to take. Wedge listened, Turnip listened, nobody heard anything but water, and they went in.

It was a great vaulted hall with whole tree trunks for pillars, its floor all mud, its far end lost in the dark. It smelled of something none of them had ever smelled before. Then, out of that dark, someone began to sing.

## The Song in the Dark

Wedge and Turnip heard singing and kept their heads. Boffo heard the sweetest voice in the world, and his eyes went soft, and he began to walk south toward it into the mud.

Wedge got in front of him and clapped his hands over Boffo's ears. Boffo shoved him away with his shield arm, which he would never have done in his right mind and did without a second thought. Turnip stood still and said the words that ward off evil things. And out of the darkness, flapping, came the singer: a filthy bird-woman, vulture from the waist down. Boffo held out his arms to her.

She took him by the shoulders, claws sunk in, and lifted. Turnip blew his last sleeping dart and it flew off into the dark. Wedge's arrow went wide. So they did the only thing left: each of them grabbed one of Boffo's legs and pulled. A fully grown hobbit in chainmail, with a sword on his back, and fed regularly, is a great deal heavier than a harpy bargains for. Boffo came loose from her grip and landed in a heap behind them.

"Guys," he said, wounded, "why did you do that?"

She came back for him, raked Turnip across the cheek on the way past, and Boffo walked straight back toward her. Wedge elbowed him. Turnip swung the statue's whip for the first time, and it cracked blue-white across her feathers and filled the hall with the smell of a summer storm. Wedge's arrows went in after it.

"Shoo!" Wedge shouted. "Go away! Boffo doesn't need pies, he's just had lunch!"

Bloodied and screeching, she flew off into the dark, and Boffo went after her.

"Guys, I'm going home with her," said Boffo, wading in deeper. "Just let me be happy for once in my life."

"No, you're not," said Wedge.

The mud was at Boffo's waist, then his chest, then his chin, and he kept on walking. Turnip threw his net once and missed. He threw it again, and caught a Boffo.

They hauled him back through the slime like a bad day's fishing while he thrashed and argued and finally drew his sword and began sawing at the mesh.

"Who carries a net around for _everything_?" he said.

He cut himself free and turned for the water again. The net survived, a little worse for it.

Wedge cupped a hand to his ear and fixed the song: dead ahead, across the water. Turnip slung a stone that way and heard it land on dry ground. Wedge cast his light, and it bloomed right in front of her face. She was crouched on a little beach on the far side of the mere, singing, lit up like a lamp in a window.

She launched herself across the water at Turnip, claws first, and caught him twice. Boffo stood beside him with his arms out, waiting to be collected. Then Wedge's darts of light and Turnip's whip hit her in the same instant. She gave one horrible scream and dropped dead on the beach.

Boffo blinked, shook his head, and was entirely himself again.

## The Harpy's Roost

Wedge read one of the healing scrolls over Turnip's torn face, and then they went to see what she had been guarding.

The mud would swallow anyone who walked across it, so Boffo, who climbed best of the three, went along the wall with a rope tied round his middle. He fell in once and was fished out, which was becoming routine, and on his second try he made the far side and tied the rope off to a pillar so the others could follow.

Her roost was a second hall, built like the first, with beams across it for perching. The floor was the worst thing any of them had seen: bones heaped everywhere, lizard and troglodyte, goblins half eaten, and at least one hobbit and possibly two. A shaft ran straight up sixty feet to a square of moonlit sky, which was how she had come and gone.

From the perches she had hung everything that glittered, the way a magpie decorates a nest. There was a necklace worth two hundred and fifty gold pieces, a scatter of bracelets and chains, and a great ruby medallion on a gold chain. Two pieces were not like the others. Wedge turned the first, a necklace, over in the lamplight and decided that whoever wore it could breathe underwater, and hung it round his own neck. The medallion, he judged, would turn aside any poison, and Boffo put it on.

They spent some time picking through the bones in case she had dropped anything she didn't fancy. She hadn't.

## The Mess Hall

The next door opened onto a dining hall, and it was a disgrace.

A long table, stools, and every mug, plate, and platter in the place dirty, used, and left lying where it fell. An oven smouldered under a stovepipe. Crates of cheap crockery lined the corridor beyond, with dried beans and rancid lard, and a couple of barrels of wine bad enough to strip paint.

"Do you suppose they never wash up," said Turnip, "and just use new ones?"

"They've got more dishes than food," said Wedge, holding his nose.

A door at the far end opened onto solid mud. Someone had hung it ready for a room nobody had dug yet.

About then the lantern guttered out. They had been underground five hours and more, and up above, the sky would soon be going grey.

## Half a Hobbit

On the way back Turnip saw something under the brown water at the edge of a flooded passage, and the lantern made it out: a pair of hobbit legs, whole as far as the waist. There was no more of him than that.

"There's a slime in there," said Wedge, "until proven otherwise."

"A pudding," Boffo agreed. "Something gelatinous. An ooze."

There was a door on the far side of that water. Nobody went to try it.

They came back past the goblins they had killed on the way in, which had lain where they fell long enough to want seeing to. So the Toad Stompers took them by the wrists and ankles and heaved them one by one into the water.

"Nasty business," said Boffo. "They don't put these parts in the stories, do they?"

"You never hear how the hero wipes his bum in the forest either, eh?" said Wedge.

## Rat Stompers

The smell of the dead brought the rats: four of them, each as big as a terrier, noses working. Wedge waved Turnip ahead with a little bow.

"Age before beauty."

"Pearls before swine," said Turnip, and missed. So did Wedge.

Boffo did not. The new sword cut the first rat clean in two. The other three came at him together and got their teeth in, and when Wedge shot one they stayed anyway, hungrier than they were afraid. Turnip's whip and Boffo's sword finished the rest.

"The Toad Stompers," Wedge declared, "are also Rat Stompers."

"Reluctant exterminators," said Turnip.

## Conclusion

The Toad Stompers have killed Derek Desleigh, robbed a harpy of her hoard, and learned that Zacharias keeps his things in the dig and goes somewhere below it. Dawn is coming and the dead will soon be missed, so the party, spent and bitten, must choose between barring themselves into Derek's snug quarters to press on and wading a day's march out of the Rushmoors to heal and return.
